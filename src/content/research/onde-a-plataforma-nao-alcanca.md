## 1 Context

The starting point is the 2025 indicators from **SaferNet**, the **Brazilian
Public Security Forum (FBSP)** and **TIC Kids Online**, which show growth in
online grooming, sextortion and the non-consensual sharing of intimate
images involving adolescents.

## 2 A gap in the legislation

The paper examines **Law 14.811/2024**, the **ECA Digital** (Brazil's
digital Child and Adolescent Statute) and **Law 15.487/2026**. These laws
address the punishment of these crimes and the regulation of platforms, but
not prevention with adolescents themselves.

## 3 Literature review

The review covers the psychosocial harm caused by these crimes, risk and
protective factors, and gender differences.

## 4 Threat model

Inspired by information security, the paper proposes a threat model that
organizes these crimes into **six stages**. The model shows that platforms
have almost no control over the **bonding** and **isolation** stages. At
that point, protection depends on the adolescents themselves, which
justifies action by schools.

## 5 Adapting to the local context

The work includes an interview guide for professionals in the Federal
District's child protection network, to adapt the model to the local
context.

## 6 Proposed intervention

A **50-minute** psychoeducational session with 8th and 9th grade students,
built around four skills:

- recognizing manipulation;
- saying no;
- asking for help;
- acting as a responsible bystander.

## 7 Evaluation

The intervention is evaluated with a questionnaire **applied before and
after** the activity.

## 8 Limitations

The paper itself points to the short dose of the intervention, the
immediate-only evaluation and the scarcity of Brazilian studies on the
topic as limitations.

## Current stage

Paper in preparation. **No data has been collected yet.**
